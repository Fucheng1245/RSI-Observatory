# RSI Timeline

A selective history of ideas and systems that shaped modern Recursive Self-Improvement. Dates refer to publication/release periods rather than a claim that one work alone created a field.

## 1960s–2000s: intelligence explosion and self-reference

**1965 — I. J. Good, “Speculations Concerning the First Ultraintelligent Machine.”** Introduces the classic intelligence-explosion argument: a machine better than humans at machine design could trigger accelerating improvement.

**1987 — Jürgen Schmidhuber, self-referential/meta-evolution work.** Early exploration of learning systems that improve learning mechanisms.

**2002–2004 — Optimal Ordered Problem Solver.** Reuses previous solutions to accelerate later search.

**2003–2006 — Gödel Machines.** Formal self-referential machines that can rewrite themselves after proving a modification improves expected utility.

## 2010s: bounded RSI, open-ended search and learned optimizers

**2011–2013 — POWERPLAY.** Continually searches for new unsolved tasks and modifies a solver while preserving prior capabilities.

**2013 — Bounded Recursive Self-Improvement.** Explores implemented goal-directed self-modeling under bounded assumptions.

**2015 — Seed AI / recursively self-improving software formulations.** Revisits RSI as a software-engineering and computational problem.

**2015 — MAP-Elites.** Quality-diversity archives become an important building block for open-ended search.

**2016 — Learning to Learn by Gradient Descent by Gradient Descent.** Meta-learns an optimizer, illustrating that the update rule itself can be learned.

**2018 — Formal RSI efficiency analysis.** Studies computability and efficiency of restricted recursive improvement.

**2019 — POET and AI-GAs.** Open-ended systems co-evolve environments/solutions and propose automated generation of learning systems.

## 2020–2023: self-training and executable optimization loops

**2020 — AutoML-Zero.** Evolves machine-learning algorithms from primitive operations.

**2022 — STaR.** Bootstraps reasoning through generated rationales, filtering and retraining.

**2023 — Self-Instruct / self-improvement with pseudo-labels.** Model-generated data becomes a standard component of iterative training.

**2023 — OPRO, Promptbreeder, STOP, DSPy.** A wave of systems treats prompts/programs/scaffolds as optimizable objects. STOP is especially notable for self-referential scaffolding improvement.

## 2024–2025: agentic self-improvement and automated research

**2024 — Self-Rewarding LMs and self-play fine-tuning.** Models increasingly generate their own feedback and preference data.

**2024 — Automated Design of Agentic Systems.** Meta-agents search executable agent architectures.

**2024 — The AI Scientist.** Automated research becomes a public, end-to-end artifact rather than only a concept.

**2025 — Self-Adapting Language Models / SEAL.** Models generate update data and adaptation directives.

**2025 — A Self-Improving Coding Agent.** Source-code modification of the agent becomes an empirical software-engineering research direction.

**2025 — Darwin Gödel Machine.** Open-ended archive-based evolution of self-improving agents combines self-modification with empirical selection.

**2025 — AlphaEvolve.** LM code generation plus automated evaluation and evolutionary search demonstrates high-value algorithm discovery.

## 2026: RSI becomes an empirical ecosystem

**Early 2026 — Automated AI R&D expands.** AI Scientist-v2, execution-grounded automated research, FT-Dojo and MLEvolve broaden the research loop from paper writing to executable ML development.

**2026 — Self-evolving agents become a survey category.** Multiple surveys formalize model, memory, tools, harnesses and feedback loops as distinct self-improvement surfaces.

**Mid-2026 — OpenRSI / Frontis-MA1.** AI4AI and ML-engineering environments are explicitly positioned toward measurable RSI.

**Mid-2026 — MetaSkill-Evolve and AREX.** Research shifts from one-level improvement toward meta-skills and outer improvement loops.

**Summer 2026 — PAST-Bench.** Evaluation focuses on whether retained experience causally improves future agent behavior.

**Summer 2026 — AI4AI-Bench.** Benchmarks algorithmic design: improving the training process rather than just task outputs.

**Summer 2026 — RSI-Exam / Scale RSI Bench.** Executable research and AI-R&D capability become explicit benchmark targets.

**August 2026 — Recuris.** Long-horizon memory/harness systems use execution evidence for persistent recursive updates.

**2026 — Embodied self-improvement accelerates.** ASPIRE, ENPIRE, RISE, MineEvolve and skill-harness evolution extend persistent improvement into robotics/physical environments.

**2026 — Safety becomes loop-specific.** Work on misevolution, tamper resilience, goal drift and safeguarded high-order objectives focuses on risks caused by repeated persistent modification.

## What changed conceptually?

The meaning of RSI shifted through four stages:

1. **Theory:** intelligence explosion and provable self-modification.
2. **Meta-learning:** improve optimizers, algorithms and learning systems.
3. **Agentic engineering:** memory, tools, prompts, code and harnesses become mutable.
4. **Automated R&D:** agents operate on the AI-development process itself, making loop closure an empirical question.

The open question is no longer whether “self-improvement” can exist in small bounded forms. It is **how far the loop can close, generalize and remain controllable across generations**.
